01 · Executive Summary

Airbnb represents one of the most compelling marketplace businesses of the last decade: a two-sided platform with genuine network effects, a 75% gross margin, and a demonstrably improving take rate. The business had FCF-positive years in FY2017–FY2019, and the structural shift toward alternative accommodation is real and durable.

However, at the $68 IPO price, the market was not buying the business, it was buying the narrative. Our five-year DCF, anchored to UNWTO tourism recovery scenarios, WACC of 6.79%, and conservative margin assumptions, yields a base-case intrinsic value of $40.28/share. Even under an aggressive bull case (rapid vaccine rollout, 90%+ domestic recovery, 35% terminal EBITDA margin), fair value reaches only $65.04, still below IPO price.

The Day-1 open of $146 (+115% above IPO price) suggests the market was pricing in a post-COVID platform supercycle that no bottom-up model could support at the time. This is a textbook case of sentiment-driven IPO pricing detaching from fundamental value.
02 · Business Overview

Revenue Model

Platform fee charged to hosts (~3%) and guests (~12–14%). Take rate grew from 11.4% in FY2015 to 14.1% in 9M 2020, evidence of increasing pricing power.

Scale

326.9M nights booked in FY2019 across 4M+ listings in 220 countries. Gross Booking Value of $38.0B. Revenue of $4.8B. COVID cut bookings 41% in FY2020E.

Unit Economics

75% gross margin (stable 2015–2020). ADR rose from $111 to $124 despite COVID, a mix shift to rural and premium properties. FCF positive in 2017–2019.

03 · Investment Thesis

▲ Bull Case Drivers

  • ▲ Platform moat deepening. Marketing spend fell from 43% to 22% of revenue (2015–2020), indicating brand is sustaining demand without paid acquisition, where Booking Holdings spends 35%+.
  • ▲ Domestic STR outperforming. US domestic STR demand was +24% vs 2019 by H1 2021 (AirDNA). Work-from-anywhere extended stays; average stay length +6.2% since 2019.
  • ▲ Take rate structurally higher. Rose from 11.4% (2015) to 14.1% (2020). Hosts have limited alternatives; pricing power is real and not competed away.
  • ▲ Capital light, FCF-generative at scale. $505M FCF in FY2018. Structural FCF margin of 13.8% in peak year, no physical assets on balance sheet.

▼ Bear Case / Key Risks

  • ▼ Valuation implies ~$100B EV at IPO. Day-1 open at $146 implies EV/Rev 2019 of 20x against Booking Holdings at 5.4x. Requires sustained hyper-growth to justify.
  • ▼ Profitability timeline is uncertain. Adj. EBITDA was -$253M in FY2019 (heavy investment year) and -$230M in 9M 2020. GAAP net income never positive pre-IPO.
  • ▼ COVID recovery pace is the entire model. Revenue fell 32% YoY in 9M 2020. Base case assumes UNWTO 50%-of-2019 international arrivals recovery in 2021, highly uncertain as of Dec 2020.
  • ▼ Post-IPO dilution and leverage. $2B emergency debt (9.5%/15.1% tranches) + heavy SBC at IPO. FCF conversion will be penalised for several years.

Methodology

Airbnb is a structurally excellent business: platform economics, strong gross margins, genuine moat. But the $68 IPO price and $146 Day-1 open reflect sentiment, scarcity of newly-listed quality growth names, and post-COVID optimism that a bottom-up DCF cannot validate. Our base case implies the stock needed to fall to $40 for it to be fairly priced on fundamentals.

This analysis demonstrates a core principle of pre-IPO valuation: DCF models set a floor, not a ceiling, for high-growth consumer platforms in a low rate, risk-on environment. The analyst's job is to understand where the market is pricing in narrative, and whether that narrative has a reasonable path to fruition.
