Learn: DDM Concepts

📌 When is DDM appropriate? ▾

DDM works best for dividend-paying companies with predictable payouts: utilities, REITs, mature financials. It's less useful for growth stocks that reinvest earnings. The key insight: dividends represent the cash a shareholder actually receives, making them a clean, direct measure of value.

⚠️ The r > g constraint ▾

GGM breaks down when g ≥ r, the denominator becomes zero or negative. In practice, g should be ≤ long-run GDP growth (~2–4% for developed markets). If you need to model a higher g, use the Two-Stage DDM instead to model a finite supernormal period first.

🔢 Implied Return formula ▾

Re-arranging GGM: r = D₁/P₀ + g. The required return = dividend yield + growth. This is the implied return an investor gets if buying at the market price. Useful to back-solve what growth the market is pricing in for a given stock.

〰️ H-Model vs Two-Stage ▾

Two-Stage DDM uses a hard cut-off: high growth for N years, then stable. H-Model assumes a smooth linear decline over 2H years. The H-Model is often more realistic for real companies where competitive advantages erode gradually, though it introduces more estimation error in H itself.
